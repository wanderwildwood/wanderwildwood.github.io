Signing in

Type your address and Mailbox looks up how to reach your provider; Gmail, iCloud and Zoho are already known to it. It opens on your address, with no welcome page.

Any server that speaks IMAP or POP3 and SMTP with a password works. There is no Sign in with Google or Microsoft: Gmail, Yahoo, AOL and Fastmail take an app password instead, made in the account's own security settings. Outlook.com no longer accepts one, so it cannot be used here.

Server settings

If nothing is found for the address, you fill the servers in by hand: protocol, server, security, port, authentication and user name.

Where mail goes

Mail goes between the phone and the servers you give it, and nowhere else. Setting up an account also asks the address's own domain, and Mozilla's list of providers, how to reach it; Mozilla is told the domain, not the address.

Pictures in a message are not fetched unless you ask, for one message or for a whole account.

A page at a time

A swipe turns one page and stops, in the inbox, in a message and in settings. Nothing scrolls on its own and nothing animates. In the inbox, the message the foot cuts through shows there and opens the next page whole; in a message, the line the old page cut through opens the next one; in settings, a page holds whole rows.

It is black and white, in Lato, with no avatars and no icons on settings rows. Dialogs are white inside a black rim and never gray out the screen behind them. Dark mode is a switch in Settings, or follows the phone's dark setting, and pictures in a message keep their colors.

Push

With an account's folders set to push, Mailbox keeps a connection open to the server so mail shows up as it arrives, and Android keeps a notification up while it does.

That notification can be hidden: Configure notification on the Push info page opens its settings, and push keeps working with it off. For mail that need not come at once, Disable push on the same page checks every so often instead, and no notification is needed.

On the lock screen

With Glance installed, how much mail is unread shows on the lock screen, beside Messaging's count. The switch is in Settings.

Licenses

About lists the app and then every library it bundles, each with its own license.

Get it

Download the APK and open it, or add the repository to Obtainium to keep it up to date. Mailbox needs Android 8 or newer. It installs beside Thunderbird rather than over it, and updates keep your accounts and mail.

On a Kompakt, add Mailbox to DuraSpeed once. The phone closes apps a few minutes after the screen goes dark, and until Mailbox is added, new mail is not looked for until you open the app. While it is at risk, Mailbox keeps a notification up that opens the right page, and you answer It's switched on once you have done it.
